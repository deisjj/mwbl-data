# DMB defense reference: runs saved by rating, in and out of position

Source: Flood013 (Albert R. Costa), *The Value of Defense including Out-of-Position Players*, Fans of Diamond Mind Baseball forum, reposted by What Cheer Wombat, May 2010.
<https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/fansofdmb/the-value-of-defense-including-out-of-position-pla-t2569.html>. All research credit to Flood013; numbers transcribed for reference. Machine-readable copy: `defense_study.json`.

## Study setup

- **Engine and era:** DMB version 9c; the 1952 season (its error rates match the neutral era), neutral parks and neutral era (about 4.8 runs per game). No DH, injuries, weather or clutch.
- **Baseline:** every player rated Av/100 at his primary position only. Each scenario re-rated the players at one position and simulated 50 seasons.
- **Units:** runs saved per season relative to that baseline, for one team using that player for every inning (about 1,450). Positive is better.
- **Range and error:** range Ex to Pr crossed with error rate 0 to 200 in steps of 25.
- **Out of position:** players rated at one position (columns) playing another (each table). The cell where the rated position equals the played position is the in-position value.
- **Arm and passed balls:** separate scenarios, varied from Av/100.
- **Noise:** about ±3 runs per cell (simulation noise). Neighboring cells aren't always monotonic.

## How score.py uses this (methodology)

1. **Rated vs. played position.** A hitter's `ratings` give his DMB rating at each position (e.g. `{"2b": "Vg/193"}`), and `pos` is where he'll play.
2. **Look up runs.** For each rated position, look up `runs[played][rated][range]` across error rates. Error rates between table columns are interpolated linearly. Past 200 the last segment is extrapolated, capped at 300. The study found that **only the player's best rated position matters** out of position, so the maximum over his rated positions is used. A player with no rating anywhere gets the table's `Unrated` value.
3. **Arm.** Outfielders (playing LF/CF/RF) add the outfield arm row for the played position when they have an `OF:` rating. Catchers add the catcher arm row and the passed-ball row. Out-of-position outfielders keep their arm rating; catchers out of position don't.
4. **Convert to WAR.** `WAR = runs × (PA / 650) × DMB_ERA_SCALE / RUNS_PER_WIN`. PA/650 scales the full-season table to the player's playing time. `DMB_ERA_SCALE` shrinks neutral-era runs toward modern scoring (default 0.93 ≈ 4.45/4.8 R/G). `RUNS_PER_WIN` = 9.83.
5. **Positional adjustment.** The study's values are relative to an average fielder *at the played position*. Difficulty across positions comes from the FanGraphs positional adjustment, which `score.py` re-bases to the played position (`pos_runs`). Both are needed: the position adjustment says how hard the job is, and the table says how well this player does it.
6. **Pitchers.** No fielding adjustment (league practice).

**Worked example: Jazz Chisholm (rated 2B Vg/193) at 3B.** In the *Playing Third Base* table, column 2B, row Vg, the error-175 cell is −25 and the error-200 cell is −34, so 193 interpolates to −31.5 runs. Over 532 PA × 0.93 era scale ÷ 9.83 that's **−2.4 WAR**, against 0 for an Av/100 third baseman. At 2B, his rated position, Vg/193 is −7.2 runs, or −0.6 WAR.

## Takeaways from the study

- **3B and the outfield** are hurt far more by out-of-position players than SS is. C and SS are the only positions where no out-of-position player, at any rating, comes out ahead of an average rated player.
- **Outfielders** are hurt more by poor range than by high error rates. For catchers and infielders, especially middle infielders, the reverse is true.
- **In position, range matters most at 3B and in the outfield.** An Ex/100 at 3B saves 27 runs, but an Ex/100 at SS saves only 15.
- **A shortstop can move to 2B or 3B cheaply** (for example, SS Ex/100 at 3B saves 14). A second baseman at 3B loses about a grade of range plus more errors.
- **A first baseman's best out-of-position spot is 2B**, then 3B, SS and the corner OF. All of them are negative.
